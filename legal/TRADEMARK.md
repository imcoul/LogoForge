## 1. Why this document exists at all

AGPL-3.0 is a copyright licence. **It grants no trademark rights whatsoever** — it says so
itself, and every other free software licence says the same. So without this page there is no
statement of what you may call things, only silence, and silence gets resolved by whoever
argues hardest.

This matters more here than in most projects. Because we deliberately refuse to gate features
(**D-010**), the source carries no lock at all. **The name is the only thing that
distinguishes the instance we run and stand behind from an identical copy run by someone
else** — and that distinction is what protects *users*, not us. Somebody who forks this code,
adds tracking, and calls the result by our name has not violated the licence. They have
violated this policy, and this policy is the reason we can stop them.

Trademark law exists to prevent confusion about **origin**. That is the whole test applied
below.

**Say true things.** You never need our permission for accurate, descriptive reference:

- "Built with {{PRODUCT_NAME}}" on work you made with the tool. **Everything you create with it

  "{{PRODUCT_NAME}} Cloud" or anything that reads as an official edition.
- Using the logo unmodified to link to the project, at a size where it is legible.
- Anything **nominative fair use** allows under applicable law. Where this policy is stricter

## 3. What needs a different name

**A modified version may not carry the Marks.** If you change the code and distribute the
result, rename it. This is the same rule Firefox, WordPress and Rust apply, and it protects the
user who downloads something with our name on it and reasonably expects our behaviour.

Concretely, rename when:

- You distribute a **fork** with functional changes.
- You run a **public service** on a modified version. *(Note that AGPL §13 separately obliges
  you to publish your modified source to that service's users. The two obligations are
  independent: complying with the AGPL does not grant you the name.)*
- You publish a **package** — npm, PyPI, a distro package, an app store listing — of a modified
  build.

**Naming your fork.** `MyThing`, or `MyThing, based on {{PRODUCT_NAME}}`. Not
`{{PRODUCT_NAME}} Pro`, `{{PRODUCT_NAME}}++`, `Open{{PRODUCT_NAME}}`, or
`{{PRODUCT_NAME}}-NG`. The test is simple: **would a reasonable person think we made it, or
approved it?**

Distro packagers applying only build, packaging and security patches may keep the name. That is
the customary exception and we have no reason to depart from it.

## 4. What is never permitted

1. **Using the Marks as, or within, your own product, company or service name** — including
   domains, social handles, app-store listings and package names.
2. **Implying endorsement, affiliation, partnership or certification** where none exists.
3. **Registering the Marks**, or anything confusingly similar, as a trademark, domain or handle
   in any jurisdiction. This includes translations and transliterations.
4. **Modifying the logo** — recolouring, redrawing, stretching, adding elements, or combining it
   with another mark to form a new one.
5. **Using the Marks on merchandise** for sale without written permission.
6. **Using the Marks in a way that suggests we endorse** a political position, a religious
   position other than our own stated one, or the conduct of a third party.
7. **Attaching the Marks to something that contradicts what they stand for** — a build with
   telemetry added, a hosted copy with tracking, a fork with dark patterns. This is the case
   this policy is really for.

## 5. Using the Marks correctly

- Spelling and capitalisation as written here: **{{PRODUCT_NAME}}**, **Srvel**, **DevNoder**,
  **Opstimizer**. Not all-caps, not all-lowercase, not hyphenated.
- **Adjective, not noun or verb.** "the {{PRODUCT_NAME}} editor", not "a {{PRODUCT_NAME}}" or
  "to {{PRODUCT_NAME}} something". Marks used as common nouns stop being marks.
- Do not pluralise or possessivise the mark itself.
- Where prominence warrants it, attribute on first use:
  > {{PRODUCT_NAME}} is a trademark of {{LEGAL_ENTITY}}.
- Keep clear space around the logo and do not place it on a background that compromises
  legibility. Colour and spacing rules are in the Srvel Brand Guide.

## 6. Products carry the endorsement, per D-011

Srvel is an **endorsed house**: products have distinctive names and are always visibly
**"by Srvel"**. Two consequences for third parties:

- The correct form is **{{PRODUCT_NAME}}**, never `Srvel {{PRODUCT_NAME}}`. That prefixed form
  is not how we write it and using it misrepresents the brand.
- **"Built by Srvel"** attribution is ours to make. Do not reproduce it on a fork, since a fork
  is by definition not built by us.

**We will write to you first, in plain language, and tell you what the problem is and what
would resolve it.** You will get a reasonable period to fix it. Nobody's first contact from us
will be a lawyer's letter — a company whose stated position is *no dark patterns* does not get
to open with intimidation.

That courtesy is not open-ended. Where a use is deliberately deceptive, or continues after
we have explained the problem, we will enforce the Marks. Not enforcing them at all would
eventually lose them, and losing them would take with it the only protection users have against
a hostile copy wearing our name.
